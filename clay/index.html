Case study · Enterprise growth

From first table to every team. Expansion as a plan, not a hope.

Most enterprise accounts land with one team and one workflow. The value is real, but it stays in that corner of the org, and renewal depends on whether one champion stays excited. What if every account had a living map from its growth priorities to its next Clay play?

Problem

Enterprise accounts reach a first win, then stall in a single team. Expansion depends on a strategist's memory and the champion's bandwidth, and the patterns across accounts rarely reach Product in a usable form.

Bet

Start every account by mapping the leader's growth priorities to concrete plays, reach a first "aha" in the kickoff itself, then keep an expansion map per team that turns into the QBR, the renewal case, and roadmap input.

How we'd know

Shorter time to first value, more teams live per account by renewal, and plays that started in one account becoming templates the next enterprise customer starts with.

01

Context

Clay gives GTM teams one place to combine data, signals, and AI research, and to act on it through campaigns and workflows. Because it's flexible, the same platform can power a RevOps team cleaning the CRM, an SDR team running signal-based outbound, and a marketing team personalizing ABM, all at once.

That flexibility is also the challenge. An enterprise customer usually starts with one of those, and nobody's job is to see the whole map. That's the Enterprise Growth Strategist's job.

02

Problem & users

For this case study I picture a Series C SaaS company (I'll call it Northwind Cloud) that is moving upmarket. RevOps bought Clay to fix CRM data. Sales and marketing have heard of it, but haven't seen what it could do for them.

GTM leader (VP RevOps)

"I bought this to fix one problem. I need to show the board it's moving pipeline, not just cleaning data."

Pain: value that's hard to see beyond one team.

Enterprise growth strategist

"I know three other teams here could use Clay. I just don't have a structured way to sequence it."

Pain: expansion lives in notes and instinct.

Clay Product team

"Strategists tell us what customers built. We need to know which patterns repeat across accounts."

Pain: anecdotes instead of evidence.

03

Why now

  • Agents raise the ceiling. As Clay moves from data to campaigns to agents, each account can do far more than it did on day one. Customers need a guide to what's newly possible.
  • Enterprises are consolidating their GTM stacks. Teams that can show multi-team value at renewal win the consolidation conversation; single-team tools get cut.
  • Every enterprise account is a template. A play that works at one fast-growing SaaS company is often exactly what the next one needs. Capturing it once compounds.

        06

        First 90 days with an account

        1. Days 1–14 · Onboard with velocity

          First aha in the kickoff. Map the leader's top priority, build the first table on their own target accounts live, and agree on the one number we'll move together.

        2. Days 15–60 · Expand with intent

          One new team at a time. Use the expansion map to sequence the next team, run a scoped pilot with a named owner, and report value in hours and pipeline, not credits.

        3. Days 61–90 · Codify and renew

          Turn wins into templates. Present the QBR as the next proposal, publish the winning play as a playbook for future enterprise customers, and bring cross-account patterns to Product.

        07

        Risks & open questions

        Tradeoffs I'd watch

        • Expanding before the first win sticks. A second team should only start once the first workflow runs without us.
        • Champion overload. Each new team needs its own owner; the original champion can't carry every rollout.
        • Credit surprises. Share usage forecasts early so growth never feels like a bill.
        • One-off builds. Custom work that can't become a template doesn't scale the program.

        Questions I'd bring to the team

        • Which plays most often become the second team's entry point today?
        • Where do enterprise accounts stall most: onboarding, the first expansion, or renewal?
        • How do strategists share playbooks today, and what would make a template worth reusing?
        • How should cross-account patterns reach Product, and how often?

Case study · Human data campaigns

Catching ambiguity before it becomes rework

A data campaign is only as good as the shared understanding behind it. When AI trainers read an instruction two different ways, the disagreement usually surfaces after batches are delivered, and the cost is relabeling, recalibration, and days lost before a training run or a new eval.

Problem

Every campaign passes through three translations: researcher intent to written instructions, instructions to vendor training, training to thousands of individual judgments. Ambiguity at any step is invisible until review, and the trainers who noticed it had no fast way to say so.

Bet

Measure agreement on hidden gold tasks by instruction section, let trainers flag an unclear rule in one click, and give the program manager a hotspot view with AI-drafted clarifications that researchers approve and vendors roll out with a quick recalibration.

How we'd know

Higher share of labels accepted without rework, gold-task agreement that recovers within hours of an instruction fix instead of after review, and steady throughput without a worse trainer experience.

01

Context

OpenAI's Human Data team turns human feedback into signals for training and evaluation, through bespoke data campaigns, synthetic data generation, and signals embedded in products. Program managers sit at the center of a campaign. They gather requirements from researchers, write the instructions, define success criteria, work with external vendors and AI trainers, calibrate those trainers, and review labeled data before it reaches a research team.

02

Problem & users

Most quality problems in a campaign aren't careless work. They're two careful people reading the same rule differently. Take a pairwise comparison of coding answers where both responses are correct, one short and one thorough. If the instructions say "prefer concise" without saying what to do when the longer answer is more complete, trainers split close to 50/50. Each trainer is consistent, the data isn't, and nobody finds out until a researcher reviews the batch.

AI trainer

"Both answers are right. The doc says prefer concise, but the long one is more helpful. I'll guess."

Pain: no fast way to ask, and a wrong guess counts against their quality score.

Researcher

"I need 12,000 labels I can trust by Friday. I find out the instructions were read two ways when the eval looks off."

Pain: late signal, rework, and a delayed training run.

Vendor lead

"My team follows the doc. When it changes, I have to retrain 400 people and confirm they all got it."

Pain: changes arrive as long emails, with no way to check recalibration.

03

Why now

  • The tasks are getting harder. As models improve, the most valuable data comes from expert judgment on subtle, high-stakes cases, which is exactly where instructions are easiest to read two ways.
  • More vendors, trainers, and campaigns run at once. A single ambiguous sentence gets multiplied across every vendor and every trainer working the same task.
  • Research moves fast. A week of rework on a data campaign is a week of delay for a training run or a new evaluation.

    05

    Metrics

    Hypotheses I'd validate with real baselines before setting targets.

    North star

    Usable data rate

    Share of delivered labels that researchers accept without rework. If ambiguity gets caught early, this rises campaign over campaign.

    Input

    Gold-task agreement by section

    Agreement on hidden gold tasks, broken out by instruction section, so a weak rule shows up on its own.

    Input

    Flag → fix time

    Hours from an ambiguity hotspot to a published, researcher-approved clarification.

    Outcome

    On-time campaigns and throughput

    Campaigns delivered by the research deadline, and accepted labels per trainer hour.

    Guardrail

    Trainer experience and cost

    Time per task, trainer satisfaction, and cost per accepted label. The system fails if trainers feel watched instead of helped.

    06

    Rollout

    1. Phase 1 · Instrument

      Gold tasks by instruction section. Seed hidden gold tasks into one live campaign and measure agreement per section. Nothing changes for trainers yet.

    2. Phase 2 · Ask

      One-click flags and a hotspot view. Add the unclear-instruction flag for trainers and the hotspot view for program managers across two or three campaigns and their vendors.

    3. Phase 3 · Close the loop

      AI-drafted clarifications and recalibration. Draft clarifications with worked examples, version every instruction change, and send a short calibration quiz to trainers whenever a rule changes.

    07

    Risks & open questions

    Tradeoffs I'd watch

    • Gaming gold tasks. Rotate them, keep them hidden, and never show trainers which tasks are graded.
    • Over-flagging. Cluster flags by instruction section so volume becomes a signal instead of noise.
    • AI changing the intent. A researcher approves every clarification before it ships.
    • Vendor and trainer privacy. Share agreement with vendors in aggregate, not individual trainer scores.

    Questions I'd bring to the team

    • How often do campaigns need rework today, and what usually causes it?
    • Are gold tasks already in use, and how are they built and refreshed?
    • How do vendors roll out instruction changes to their trainers now?
    • Which campaigns are most sensitive to ambiguity: expert domains, safety, or agentic tasks?

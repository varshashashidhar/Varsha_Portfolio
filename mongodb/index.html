Case study · Atlas consumption forecasting

Forecasting Atlas from one trusted number

Atlas revenue follows how much customers actually use across AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure. That makes the Atlas consumption forecast one of the most important numbers MongoDB's Finance team produces, and one of the hardest to assemble when usage, commitments, credits, and marketplace billing each live in a different place.

Problem

FP&A builds the Atlas forecast from manual pulls of usage, commitment drawdowns, credits, and marketplace billing. Each pull defines revenue a little differently, so a growing share of every cycle goes to reconciling sources instead of explaining why consumption moved.

Bet

Publish certified, near-real-time consumption datasets in the lakehouse, let analysts flag a mismatch from inside their forecast model, and feed AI-driven forecasts only from certified inputs, broken down by cloud, region, and cluster tier.

How we'd know

A higher share of forecast inputs from certified data, fewer manual pulls per cycle, faster time from a flagged mismatch to a certified fix, and lower forecast error against actual Atlas consumption.

01

Context

Atlas is billed on usage. What a customer pays depends on the cluster tiers they run, the clouds and regions they deploy in, and how their annual commitment and credits are drawn down. Some customers buy through the AWS, Google Cloud, or Azure marketplaces, which report on their own schedules. The Internal Data team turns that high-volume consumption data into the datasets, dashboards, and APIs Finance uses for planning, forecasting, and spend efficiency. I'm assuming a lakehouse with medallion layers, as the role describes: raw usage events land in bronze, get cleaned and joined to contracts in silver, and become certified financial tables in gold.

02

Problem & users

Consumption changes every day, so Finance can't wait for month-end close to see the trend. When certified data is late, hard to find, or doesn't break out what an analyst needs, they pull it themselves, and each pull makes its own choices: invoice date or usage date for credits, gross or net of marketplace fees, which exchange rate. The forecast still gets done. But every cycle starts with a reconciliation, and the reason each number differs never reaches the team that owns the data.

FP&A analyst

"Guidance prep starts Monday. My Atlas pull and the Finance dashboard are $1.3M apart for September, and I don't know which one leadership has seen."

Pain: deadline pressure, and no fast way to know which number is right.

Finance leader

"If Atlas consumption slows in a region, I need to see it within days, and trust the number I'm explaining."

Pain: trends show up late, and reviews start with reconciliation.

Data engineer

"I learn a definition drifted when two reports disagree in a leadership review."

Pain: reactive fixes, with no ranked view of what Finance needs most.

03

Why now

  • Atlas consumption shapes guidance. Consumption trends are a regular part of how MongoDB explains its results. The sooner Finance sees a shift by cloud, region, or segment, and the more it trusts that number, the better the forecast behind those conversations.
  • AI forecasting needs one definition first. The role's goal is automated, AI-driven forecasting. A model trained on three versions of consumption revenue learns all three, so certification has to come before automation.
  • Data volume and SOX keep growing. As a public company with a fast-growing financial data footprint, every manual pull is a control risk as well as a time cost.

  { $merge: { into: "fin_atlas_consumption", whenMatched: "replace" } }
])

    Design choice: the engineer gets the flag with both numbers, the lineage, and the failing reconciliation check, so the definition is fixed once in the gold step. I wrote that step as a MongoDB aggregation pipeline to show the logic; in a lakehouse it would be the equivalent SQL or Spark job.

    Hypotheses I'd validate with real baselines before setting targets.

    North star

    Share of Atlas forecast inputs from certified data

    Percent of the consumption, commitment, and credit figures in FP&A's Atlas forecast that trace to a certified gold dataset instead of a manual pull. If the loop works, this rises every cycle.

    Input

    Days to see the trend

    How many days after usage happens FP&A can see trusted consumption by cloud, region, and tier.

    Input

    Flag → certified time

    Median days from a recurring mismatch to a certified definition.

    Outcome

    Forecast error vs. actual Atlas consumption

    Quarterly forecast error, plus analyst hours spent reconciling during guidance prep.

    Guardrail

    Finance velocity and controls

    Time to deliver the forecast, and SOX control exceptions. The product fails if certification slows guidance prep or weakens controls.

    06

    Rollout

    1. Phase 1 · Listen

      Shadow one Atlas forecast cycle. Sit with FP&A through guidance prep, inventory every manual pull, and rank the definitions that cause the most reconciliation time.

    2. Phase 2 · Certify

      Certify daily consumption, commitments, and credits. Publish gold datasets with owners, tests, freshness targets, and reconciliation to invoiced billing. Add one-click flags to the dashboards and spreadsheets FP&A already uses.

    3. Phase 3 · Automate

      AI-driven Atlas forecasts. Baseline forecasts by cloud, region, and cluster tier that read only certified data, with AI-drafted explanations of what moved and a person signing off before anything reaches leadership.

    07

    Risks & open questions

    Tradeoffs I'd watch

    • Marketplace and currency timing. Show estimates for days the clouds haven't reported yet, clearly labeled, and true them up when billing lands.
    • Analysts trust their own models. Meet FP&A in their tools and show the reconciliation, rather than asking them to give up their spreadsheets.
    • Noisy AI explanations. Root causes cite lineage and show confidence, and a person certifies every change.
    • Sensitive data and SOX. Access follows existing Finance permissions, and each certified metric has one named owner and a logged change history.

    Questions I'd bring to the team

    • Which definitions cause the most reconciliation during guidance prep today?
    • How many days behind real usage does FP&A see trusted consumption now?
    • How much forecast error comes from data quality versus the forecasting model itself?
    • Could the same certified layer support AR aging and workforce planning next?
